
## Steward

The price migration engine is the in-house complex robot that performs the task of bringing a cohort of subscriptions from one set of prices to another. Migrations are simple in theory, but in practice are delicate and long running processes, and we often have several of them running in parallel. 
It's a unique system because it operates under operational rules that are very different from the other systems that the teams in P&E own and maintain. Important differences include the lack of CODE environment (impossible in practice due to legal constraints), the fact that it's a slow process lasting 13 months per migration, but then where everyday is a legally mandated deadline. Much more importantly, mistakes carry a significant reputational risk for the guardian (breaking the guardian dot com for 20 mins because of a bad deploy or a runtime bug is nothing in comparison). It is also, this needs mentioning, of strategic importance for the Guardian financial projections.

Because of its complexity and the interdependency of its different internal parts, but also the heterogeneity of the systems it connects to and orchestrate, it requires very specific skills and expertise (notably a great amount of institutional knowledge). Since late 2022, Pascal has embodied and then defined that function and calls it engine steward.

Being an engine steward only requires part time efforts (the steward can happily work on other projects) but is a full time responsibility, which manifests in being available daily to investigate and fix any problem with the ongoing migrations (once a migration is in automatic pilot the migration only runs in the morning and alarms happen only then [1]), but also being available to interface with the stakeholders (Marketing and Finance) for providing answers and occasionally advice on what is possible or not (often, but not always, feature requests for coming migrations -- the user optout logic of the October 2025 product migration is a great example of that). Implementing a specific feature never really takes a lot of time, but requires a nearly complete understanding of the engine as a whole (in part for not breaking existing migrations). The engine itself is written in Scala/ZIO, but the steward will develop expertise in Zuora, a small understanding of Salesforce (here Graham helps a lot), AWS Dynamo, and a bit of understanding of Braze. In addition of this, Pascal also has developed an extensive suite of personal tools that help with investigations, enhance observability, and allow pre migration batch configurations/checks, or deterministic repairs when needed.

[1] This doesn't means it happens all the time. In some periods the engine works for months without a single alarm and in other periods, when we go through a "rough patch of subscriptions" (often, incorrect/incomplete/unexpected static data in Zuora or Salesforce) it can alarm more often.

The only meeting requirement is the weekly Marketing catchup (dedicated to migrations). 

One interesting aspect of the engine is the fact that it doesn't have a roadmap or the need for a product person (with that said Marketing has an impressive migration roadmap). The task is always the same from one migration to another, but the details of a given migration can be deep in their complexity. Here, the steward(s) have the opportunity, freedom, and duty, to refactor and maintain the engine's code, layout and internal coherence in the face of evolving migration specifications [2]. Occasionally, changes are mandated by modifications in the interfaces with the systems it connects to (for instance the implementation of the Zuora Orders API, or designing better cohort schemas in Dynamo).

[2] It's important to emphasis that this is not something anybody is going to ask them to do. Stewards must operate driven by their own taste, and, here, a certain amount of autonomy, maturity as well as confidence is essential (for instance the long investigation Pascal performed in summer 2025, that led to async amendment processing). Last, but not least, the stewards must be comfortable working with deadlines (migrations often take place within a larger context of marketing campaigns that are difficult to reschedule).
